# Copart, Inc. - Porter Five Forces Stock Analysis

Prepared using [`SKILL.md`](../SKILL.md).

Data cutoff: latest quarterly report found was Copart's fiscal Q3 2026 Form 10-Q for the quarter ended April 30, 2026, filed May 29, 2026. Latest annual filing used was fiscal 2025 Form 10-K for the year ended July 31, 2025, filed September 26, 2025.

## 1. Company Snapshot

- Company: Copart, Inc., ticker CPRT, Nasdaq.
- Business: global online auctions and vehicle remarketing. Copart processes and auctions salvage, damaged, clean-title, fleet, finance, dealer, charity, rental, and individual vehicles, primarily through its VB3 online auction platform.
- Main customers and counterparties: vehicle sellers are primarily insurance companies, but also dealers, individuals, charities, rental car companies, banks, finance companies, and fleet operators. Buyers are vehicle dismantlers, rebuilders, repair licensees, used vehicle dealers, exporters, and the public.
- Geography: fiscal 2025 revenue was 83.0% U.S. and 17.0% International. Operations include the U.S., U.K., Germany, Brazil, Canada, U.A.E., Spain, Finland, Oman, Ireland, and Bahrain.
- Revenue drivers: service fees from auctions and auction-related services; vehicle sales where Copart acts as principal; unit volume; total loss frequency; average auction selling price; used-car values; repair costs; scrap/commodity values; mix of seller programs; buyer demand, including international demand.
- Revenue mix: FY2025 total revenue was $4.647 billion; service revenue was $3.969 billion, about 85%; vehicle sales were $678 million, about 15%.
- Main cost drivers: facility operations, labor, subhaul/transportation, fuel, property costs, insurance, facility depreciation, technology, marketing, G&A, and cost of vehicles sold. In FY2025, facility operations were 42% of revenue, cost of vehicle sales 13%, G&A 9%, and operating income 36%.

## 2. Relevant Industry Definition

- Product/service scope: salvage and wholesale vehicle auction, vehicle remarketing, title processing, storage, towing/logistics coordination, buyer/seller marketplace services, data/valuation tools, and adjacent vehicle disposition services.
- Geographic scope: primarily U.S. salvage vehicle remarketing, with international expansion in the U.K., Europe, Canada, Brazil, and the Middle East. Local yard coverage matters because vehicles must be towed, stored, titled, inspected, and released.
- Main competitors: RB Global/IAA, Manheim/Cox, OPENLANE, Carvana/ADESA, ACV Auctions, local/regional auctioneers, private independent remarketers, and dismantlers such as LKQ or local recycling groups that can bypass auction platforms.
- Main substitutes: insurer direct sales to dismantlers/rebuilders, repair instead of total-loss designation, dealer/wholesale marketplaces, local salvage yards, direct buyer relationships, and doing more vehicle disposition in-house.
- Main suppliers: insurance companies and other vehicle sellers; land/facility providers; labor; independent subhaulers; title/DMV processes; cloud/software and payment vendors.
- Main buyer groups: dismantlers/recyclers, rebuilders, used-car dealers, repair licensees, exporters, brokers, and public buyers where permitted.

## 3. Profitability Baseline

- ROIC proxy: FY2025 NOPAT was about $1.39 billion, using FY2025 operating income of $1.697 billion and an effective tax rate near 18.3%. Average invested capital proxy, debt plus lease liabilities plus equity minus cash/restricted cash, was about $6.32 billion, implying ROIC around 22%. This is a rough proxy because Copart holds large cash balances and reports limited debt.
- EBIT margin: FY2025 operating margin was 36.5%. Latest TTM operating margin through April 30, 2026 was also about 36.6%.
- Free cash flow margin: FY2025 operating cash flow minus capex was about $1.23 billion, or 26.5% of revenue. For the nine months ended April 30, 2026, operating cash flow was $1.247 billion and capital expenditures/acquisitions were $263 million, implying about 28.0% FCF margin on nine-month revenue.
- Revenue growth: FY2025 revenue grew 9.7% after 9.5% growth in FY2024. For the nine months ended April 30, 2026, revenue was $3.514 billion, down about 0.2% year over year, partly against catastrophe-related FY2025 comparisons.
- Capital intensity: meaningful for a marketplace because Copart needs yards and equipment. FY2025 capex was about $570 million, or 12.3% of revenue. Net PP&E was $3.60 billion at July 31, 2025 and $3.72 billion at April 30, 2026.
- Working capital/liquidity: cash, cash equivalents, and restricted cash were $3.35 billion at April 30, 2026; working capital was $4.53 billion; unused capacity under the 2026 revolving credit facility was $1.228 billion.

## 5. Overall Force Ratings

| Force | Overall pressure | Confidence | Main drivers | Trend |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| New entrants | Low | High | High capital/site needs, two-sided network effects, insurer relationships, title/logistics know-how, and Copart's 281-facility footprint. | Stable |
| Suppliers | Medium | High | Insurance sellers provide 81% of processed vehicles and are concentrated, but Copart's buyer liquidity and service quality are valuable to them. | Worsening |
| Buyers | Medium | Medium | Buyer base is broad, but buyers are professional and price sensitive because vehicles are inventory and substitutes exist. | Stable |
| Substitutes | Medium | Medium | Direct dismantler sales, repair instead of total-loss, in-house disposition, and other digital marketplaces can bypass Copart, but often lack its liquidity. | Stable |
| Rivalry | Medium | High | IAA/RB Global, Manheim, OPENLANE, Carvana/ADESA, ACV, and dismantlers compete for supply, buyers, yards, and seller economics. | Worsening |

Weighted judgment: Copart's industry structure is attractive because the physical network, seller relationships, data, buyer liquidity, and regulatory/process know-how are hard to replicate. The main offset is not easy startup entry; it is bargaining pressure from insurance sellers and rivalry from IAA/RB Global and direct dismantler channels.

## 6. Most Important Forces

- Force that matters most: suppliers. Insurance companies provide the core inventory, and seller contract economics determine volume, fee rates, and required service levels.
- Force changing fastest: rivalry. RB Global's IAA ownership, digital wholesale platforms, AI valuation tools, and direct buyer relationships make competition more data-driven and service-driven.
- Most misunderstood by investors: new entrants. The website/auction layer is easy to copy; the hard part is yard density, title handling, insurer trust, buyer liquidity, and catastrophe response.
- Are current profits protected or likely competed away? Current profits look protected by network effects, facilities, and scale. Excess returns are not risk-free, because insurer bargaining power and contract competition can take some economics over time.

## 7. Stock-Relevant Takeaways

- Pricing power: strong, but expressed indirectly through seller returns, buyer fees, service fees, storage/title/logistics services, and auction liquidity rather than simple list-price increases.
- Margin durability: high relative to auction and retail adjacencies. FY2025 operating margin was 36.5%, versus about 15.5% for RB Global and about 10.2% for OPENLANE. Durability depends on preserving buyer liquidity and insurer supply.
- Capital intensity: higher than a pure software marketplace. Capex was 12.3% of FY2025 revenue and net PP&E was $3.72 billion at April 30, 2026, but free cash flow remains strong.
- Growth quality: highest when driven by higher service revenue per car, more total-loss volume, more buyer liquidity, and international expansion. Lower quality if driven by principal vehicle sales mix or unusually high catastrophe volumes.
- Main risks: loss or repricing of major insurer supply, weaker U.S. total-loss volume, direct-to-dismantler bypass, used-car/scrap/FX pressure on buyer bids, export restrictions, higher subhaul/labor/insurance costs, yard capacity bottlenecks, and regulatory/AML/environmental issues.
- Evidence that would change the conclusion: sustained loss of insurer volume to IAA or direct channels; falling buyer participation or international demand; declining net returns to sellers; lower fee take rates; capex rising without revenue growth; or evidence that direct dismantler procurement scales nationally.

## 8. Mistakes to Avoid Check

- Avoided defining competition only as direct competitors: yes, included direct dismantler purchases, insurer direct sales, repair decisions, local yards, wholesale marketplaces, and in-house disposition.
- Avoided defining the industry too broadly/narrowly: yes, focused on salvage/wholesale vehicle remarketing with geographic and process constraints.
- Avoided making only lists: yes, each force links evidence to pressure on industry profitability.
- Avoided equal-weighting forces: yes, supplier power and rivalry are weighted more heavily than startup entry.
- Avoided unexplained buyer price sensitivity: yes, buyer pressure is tied to inventory economics, resale values, scrap prices, FX, transport, and financing.
- Avoided assuming fast growth means attractive: yes, recent FY2026 revenue was flat despite strong historical growth.
- Avoided assuming advanced technology means attractive: yes, technology is treated as both a Copart advantage and a substitute/rivalry enabler.
- Avoided treating government as a sixth force: yes, regulation is analyzed as an entry barrier, cost driver, and compliance/rivalry factor.
- Avoided treating complements as a sixth force: yes, subhaulers, insurers, brokers, and finance providers are analyzed as suppliers/intermediaries.
- Avoided relying on one-year results: yes, used FY2023-FY2025 and latest FY2026 nine-month evidence.
- Avoided assuming consolidation fixes rivalry: yes, RB Global/IAA is treated as a more committed rival, not as an automatic profitability fix.
- Avoided using the framework only to label good/bad industry: yes, the analysis links structure to pricing power, margin durability, capex, growth quality, and disconfirming evidence.
