# VeriSign (NASDAQ: VRSN) — Five Forces Analysis

**Research date and cutoff: September 6, 2026.** Annual baseline: fiscal years ended December 31, 2021–2025. Latest completed annual disclosure inspected: FY2025 10-K, filed February 5, 2026; latest interim: June 30, 2026 10-Q, filed July 23, 2026. Subsequent public developments through the cutoff are identified separately. This report is for informational purposes and is **not investment advice**; it does not estimate fair value or recommend a trade.

**Central judgment:** VeriSign operates a highly defensible registry franchise within a more competitive market for online identity. Its strongest protection is the combination of exclusive registry contracts and the cost of abandoning an established domain. That protects existing earnings better than it guarantees growth. Contractual limits, distribution dependence, and changing demand prevent it from controlling the entire ecosystem.

## 1. Company Snapshot

VeriSign operates the authoritative registries for .com and .net: it maintains registrations and the infrastructure that helps the internet locate those domains. A registry is the wholesale operator of an extension; a registrar sells registrations to end users. VeriSign also performs root-zone maintenance and operates the A and J root servers. These roles do not constitute ownership of the internet. Its business is reported as one segment, with substantially all revenue from .com and .net. Smaller registry and technical-service activities are not separately material. ## 2. Relevant Industry Definition

The relevant market is **global wholesale domain-name registry services**, with two distinct competitive settings: servicing an existing .com/.net registration under exclusive contracts, and winning a new registration against other extensions. Geography is global, although country-code domains and national regulation make local competition meaningful.

Other extensions are rivals within this industry. Social profiles, marketplace storefronts, hosted subdomains, and app-only identities are substitutes for buying one's own domain. Hosting, email, security, search, and website builders can be complements as well as alternative routes to an online presence. DNS queries are usage, not billable domain registrations.

The Q2 2026 worldwide domain base was **401.6 million**: .com/.net represented a calculated **44.6%**, country-code TLDs **37.0%**, and the remainder other generic TLDs. These are registration counts, not revenue shares or shares of web traffic. DNIB is published by VeriSign and is therefore an interested industry source. [Q2 2026 DNIB](https://investor.verisign.com/news-releases/news-release-details/dnibcom-reports-internet-has-4016-million-domain-name)

## 3. Industry Chain, Brand Power, and Ecosystem Control

End users pay registrars; registrars remit registration fees to VeriSign; VeriSign pays staff, infrastructure vendors, and contractual fees. Registration instructions flow into the registry, while delegation information supports DNS resolution. ICANN's rules and contractual rights govern access to this chain. Retail customer records, hosting workflows, and end-user content mostly sit elsewhere.

## 7. Most Important Forces

**The structure is attractive for the incumbent core and mixed for new TLD entrants.** The most important force is entry: the exclusive, conditionally renewable registry position explains why a capable competitor cannot underbid VeriSign for an unchanged .com domain. Buyer switching costs then preserve that advantage at renewal. The contract and switching mechanism receive economic weight once; the brand and ecosystem overlays explain how they operate rather than adding extra scores. [.com agreement, 2024](https://itp.cdn.icann.org/en/files/registry-agreements/com/com-agreement-html-01-12-2024-en.htm)

**Rivalry is changing fastest in observable evidence.** Flat core-domain volume over 2021–2025 contrasts with the 2026 recovery and faster growth outside the core. ICANN's new application round increases the future choice set, but the announced applications are neither approved strings nor immediate revenue threats. [2023 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1014473/000101447324000006/vrsn-20231231.htm) [2025 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1014473/000101447326000006/vrsn-20251231.htm) [Q2 2026 DNIB](https://investor.verisign.com/news-releases/news-release-details/dnibcom-reports-internet-has-4016-million-domain-name) [ICANN, August 13, 2026](https://www.icann.org/en/announcements/details/icann-2026-round-closes-with-more-than-1600-new-gtld-applications-13-08-2026-en)

An **analytical hypothesis**, not a verified claim about investor beliefs, is that registrar concentration is easy to misunderstand: retail power and registry power are different. A registrar can influence the next purchase, yet cannot change the registry beneath an existing .com name. Conversely, saying “monopoly” without defining the namespace overlooks competition for future identities.

Current profits appear **structurally protected rather than temporarily elevated by a supply shortage**. Their durability is conditional on contract compliance, reliable operations, continued renewals, and acceptable customer economics. Five-year gross margins support persistence, not proof of unlimited pricing power. The 2024 domain-base decline and the wider market's faster recent growth are material counterevidence to automatic growth claims. [2025 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1014473/000101447326000006/vrsn-20251231.htm) [Q2 2026 DNIB](https://investor.verisign.com/news-releases/news-release-details/dnibcom-reports-internet-has-4016-million-domain-name)

Relative to GoDaddy Registry, PIR, and Identity Digital, VeriSign is better protected against direct core entry and buyer substitution of suppliers, and enjoys a much larger single namespace than most alternative-extension portfolios. It is not demonstrably better placed on supplier procurement: comparable purchasing data are missing. .com gives it a broader installed identity against substitutes, while PIR has mission-specific differentiation. Against rivalry, VeriSign benefits from continuity but has less retail integration than GoDaddy and narrower exposure to new-extension growth than portfolio operators. Brand contributes to identity relevance; evidence does not separate its price premium from contracts and switching costs. [GoDaddy Registry, February 2026](https://registry.godaddy/blog/godaddy-registry-certified-for-icanns-next-round-of-new-gtlds/) [PIR 2025 impact report](https://pir.org/annual-impact-report-2025/) [Identity Digital company disclosure](https://identity.digital/company)

## 8. Stock-Relevant Takeaways

- **Pricing power is contractual and conditional.** The .com schedule permits increases in the final four pricing years of each six-year cycle; pricing years run October 26–October 25. The $10.26-to-$10.97 change announced April 23 is effective **November 1, 2026**, not already realized at the cutoff. Registration fees are recognized over service periods, so a higher price does not instantly reprice all reported revenue. [.com agreement, 2024](https://itp.cdn.icann.org/en/files/registry-agreements/com/com-agreement-html-01-12-2024-en.htm) [Q2 2026 10-Q](https://investor.verisign.com/static-files/50e1da54-ebd8-40ef-97f6-3f456b603f51)
- **A second price change is already public.** An August registrar notice reports .net moving from **$10.91 to $12.00 on March 1, 2027**, corroborated by contemporaneous trade reporting. This is post-interim evidence, not a fact taken from the June 10-Q. Future implementation and renewal behavior matter more than treating the announcement as fresh news. No assumption is made that the full gross increase becomes immediate net profit. [OSIR registrar notice](https://osir.com/en/blog/verisign-net-price-increase-2027/) [Domain Name Wire, August 27](https://domainnamewire.com/2026/08/27/verisign-announces-net-price-increase/)
- **Brand supports customer choice but its financial contribution is not isolated.** The .com identifier matters more than the corporate label. Retention and channel relevance also reflect established identity and exclusive supply; an aftermarket sale premium generally belongs to the name owner or intermediaries, not automatically VeriSign.
- **Margins are durable, with real reinvestment obligations.** Calculated gross margin rose from 85.55% to 88.15% across 2021–2025. Annual cash capex/revenue ranged from approximately 1.4% to 4.0%; this excludes expensed R&D and specialist personnel. A low capex ratio is not permission to underinvest in security. [2023 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1014473/000101447324000006/vrsn-20231231.htm) [2025 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1014473/000101447326000006/vrsn-20251231.htm)
- **Growth quality needs cohort evidence.** The core base moved from 173.4m names in 2021 to 173.5m in 2025, then 179.1m in June 2026. New registrations and renewal improvement are encouraging, but promotional or speculative names may not become durable recurring customers. Registration counts do not measure active businesses. [2023 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1014473/000101447324000006/vrsn-20231231.htm) [2025 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1014473/000101447326000006/vrsn-20251231.htm) [July 23 results](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1014473/000101447326000026/q22026earningsrelease.htm)
- **Shared ecosystem governance limits expansion of the profit pool.** VeriSign can collect the core wholesale fee while registrars, hosts, and content owners retain downstream economics. .web creates an option to compete for new identities under the base registry agreement, not evidence that the .com franchise has been replicated. [.web delegation, July 22](https://investor.verisign.com/news-releases/news-release-details/verisign-announces-delegation-web)
- **Capital allocation needs an adjusted liquidity view.** June 2026 cash/investments included proceeds from a new $550m note issue; the company redeemed the corresponding old $550m notes on July 20. Treating the entire June balance as lasting surplus cash would overstate flexibility. Buybacks and dividends compete with resilience spending and cash reserves. [July 23 results](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1014473/000101447326000026/q22026earningsrelease.htm)
- **Evidence that would change the conclusion:** sustained weaker renewal cohorts after price increases; a binding adverse change to contractual rights or price limits; a major service/security failure; or proof that growth of alternative identities structurally reduces paying domain demand. Strong .web renewals and profitable incremental demand could improve the growth outlook, but require post-launch evidence.

## 9. Mistakes to Avoid Check

The analysis separates registries from registrars, existing-name economics from new demand, and other TLD rivalry from platform substitution. It distinguishes list prices, revenue, registrations, active usage, and wholesale versus retail customer relationships. Government, complements, brand, and lifecycle are overlays or mechanisms within the five forces. The force judgments are not averaged, and maturity is not equated with safety.

Material limitations remain: unknown supplier spend and end-user mix; no isolated brand premium or brand-caused retention; no quantified incremental network effect; noncomparable peer financial models; and insufficient evidence for supplier/substitute trends. DNIB and reliability statements are company-origin evidence. The NTIA explanatory blog was **source inaccessible** because retrieval timed out; contractual conclusions instead use inspected ICANN documents. GoDaddy's annual-report PDF could not be retrieved; its inspected SEC earnings release and registry disclosure supply the narrower peer evidence used here. No blanket claim of complete industry data is made.
