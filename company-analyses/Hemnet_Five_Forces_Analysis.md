# Hemnet Group AB - Porter Five Forces Stock Analysis

Prepared using [`SKILL.md`](../SKILL.md).

Data cutoff: latest quarterly report found was Q1 2026, published 2026-04-28. Hemnet's Q2 2026 report was scheduled for 2026-07-17, so it was not yet available in the sources used.

## 1. Company Snapshot

- Company: Hemnet Group AB (publ), ticker HEM, Nasdaq Stockholm.
- Business: Swedish digital residential property marketplace. Hemnet sells listing and visibility products mainly to home sellers, plus products to real estate agents, property developers, banks, and advertisers.
- Geography: essentially Sweden; Q1 2026 report says revenue is in principle almost entirely from Swedish customers.
- Main revenue drivers: number of paid/published housing objects, ARPL, conversion to Plus/Premium/Max add-ons, B2B subscriptions/ads, and Swedish housing-market liquidity.
- Revenue mix: FY2025 revenue SEK 1,526.8m; housing-ad services SEK 1,344.9m, about 88%; other services SEK 181.9m, about 12%. By customer, home sellers were SEK 1,314.8m, about 86%; agents SEK 103.2m; developers SEK 48.1m; advertisers SEK 60.7m.
- Main cost drivers: broker administration/provision compensation, marketing, software/licenses, consultants, personnel, and capitalized product development. FY2025 broker-related administration/provision compensation was SEK 403.7m, about 26% of revenue; personnel was SEK 225.9m, about 15%; total investments were SEK 25.7m, about 1.7%.

## 2. Relevant Industry Definition

- Product/service scope: Swedish residential-property listing, search, exposure, lead generation, data/insight, and related advertising products.
- Geographic scope: Sweden. This is local because property data, broker relationships, consumer brand memory, and housing transaction norms are national/local.
- Main competitors: Booli, Boneo, broker-chain own sites/pre-market databases, Din Bostad, Hemily, social/search channels, and other performance-ad channels.
- Main substitutes: selling through broker private networks, broker websites, social media, search advertising, "coming soon" or pre-market sales, doing less paid exposure, or accepting longer sales time.
- Main suppliers: real estate agents and chains that supply listing flow and influence seller choice; software/cloud/payment vendors; labor; traffic-acquisition channels.
- Main buyer groups: home sellers, brokers, property developers, banks, advertisers, and indirectly home buyers whose traffic makes the platform valuable.

## 3. Profitability Baseline

- ROIC proxy: FY2025 EBIT SEK 673.8m x (1 - 20.7% tax) = about SEK 534m NOPAT. Average invested capital proxy, debt + equity - cash, was about SEK 1,615m, so ROIC including goodwill/acquired intangibles was about 33%. Q1 2026 rolling twelve-month EBIT was SEK 604.2m, implying about 30% ROIC on the same rough basis.
- EBIT margin: FY2025 44.1%; Q1 2026 rolling twelve-month 41.8%.
- Free cash flow margin: FY2025 operating cash flow minus investments was about SEK 572m, or 37.5% of revenue. Q1 2026 rolling twelve-month operating cash flow minus investments was about SEK 520m, or 36.0% of revenue.
- Revenue growth: FY2025 revenue grew 9.5%; Q1 2026 rolling twelve-month revenue was down to SEK 1,445.5m due to weak housing activity and the revenue-timing effect of "sell first, pay later".
- Capital intensity: very low. FY2025 total investments were 1.7% of revenue; Q1 2026 rolling twelve-month investments were 1.6%.
- Working capital: light. FY2025 working-capital change was +SEK 3.6m; Q1 2026 rolling twelve-month was +SEK 14.4m.

## 5. Overall Force Ratings

| Force | Overall pressure | Confidence | Main drivers | Trend |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| New entrants | Medium | High | Strong network effects and brand reduce pressure, but low capital needs and new entrants raise it. | Worsening |
| Suppliers | Medium | Medium | Broker chains supply and influence listing flow; Hemnet is paying/partnering more to secure that channel. | Worsening |
| Buyers | Medium | High | Sellers are fragmented and Hemnet ROI is high, but upfront price visibility, weak market conditions, and broker influence raise pressure. | Worsening |
| Substitutes | High | Medium | Booli, Boneo, broker pre-market, own sites, social/search, and cheaper new portals are credible alternatives. | Worsening |
| Rivalry | Medium | High | Hemnet remains dominant with high margins, but price-led and ecosystem-led rivalry is intensifying. | Worsening |

Weighted judgment: the strongest economic protection is Hemnet's audience/listing network effect. The main offset is not a single equal-size rival, but a coordinated squeeze from brokers, free/cheap substitutes, and seller price sensitivity. That is why the structure is still attractive, but less clean than a monopoly-like historical reading would imply.

## 6. Most Important Forces

- Force that matters most: Substitutes plus broker/supplier power. Hemnet's economics depend on being the default home-selling exposure channel. If agents and sellers believe alternatives are "good enough", ARPL and volume could weaken together.
- Force changing fastest: Substitutes. Booli/Boneo, broker partnerships, pre-market sales, social-led discovery, Hemily/Din Bostad, and AI/search tools are all moving at once.
- Most misunderstood by investors: Buyer power is not just millions of fragmented households. Brokers are both channel partners and suppliers, and can shape seller behavior before a seller reaches Hemnet.
- Are profits protected or likely competed away? Current profits are protected by brand, reach, network effects, and low capital intensity, but the excess pricing component is more contestable than the core marketplace role.

## 7. Stock-Relevant Takeaways

- Pricing power: Still strong, shown by FY2025 ARPL +28.1% despite published objects -13.3%. But Q1 2026 shows the fragility: revenue -24.7% and EBITDA margin 36.1% while the model changed and volumes stayed weak.
- Margin durability: Structurally high due low capex and software economics, but not immune. Broker compensation, marketing, product investment, and lower listing volume can reduce fixed-cost absorption.
- Capital intensity: Very attractive. FY2025 investments were only 1.7% of revenue and Q1 2026 rolling twelve-month cash generation was 98.7%.
- Growth quality: Best when ARPL growth comes from measurable seller ROI and premium conversion, not just price increases. Growth is lower quality if it depends on pushing prices while listing share leaks to substitutes.
- Main risks: loss of agent goodwill, cheaper/free substitutes gaining habit, weak Swedish housing turnover, regulatory/competition scrutiny, and ARPL growth causing volume leakage.
- Evidence that would change the conclusion: sustained paid-object recovery in H2 2026 without ARPL rollback; stable/increasing Hemnet listing share; competitors failing to convert traffic into seller-paid products; broker agreements improving conversion without large commission inflation; or independent proof that Hemnet exposure materially raises sale price/time-to-sale across markets.

## 8. Mistakes to Avoid Check

- Avoided defining competition only as direct competitors: yes, substitutes include pre-market, broker sites, social/search, and doing less paid exposure.
- Avoided defining the industry too broadly/narrowly: yes, scope is Swedish residential property listing/search/lead generation.
- Avoided making only lists: yes, each force links numbers to profitability pressure.
- Avoided equal-weighting forces: yes, network effects, substitutes, and broker power were weighted more heavily.
- Avoided unexplained buyer price sensitivity: yes, it is tied to weak housing markets, visible seller costs, and alternatives.
- Avoided assuming fast growth means attractive: yes, recent growth is partly ARPL-driven and volume is weak.
- Avoided assuming advanced tech means attractive: yes, AI/search is treated as both defense and substitute enabler.
- Avoided treating government as sixth force: yes, regulation is considered within entry/rivalry.
- Avoided treating complements as sixth force: yes, brokers are treated as suppliers/intermediaries.
- Avoided relying on one-year results: partly; used FY2025, Q1 2026 rolling data, and 2024 comparatives where available, but competitor-share evidence is mostly 2025-2026 secondary reporting.
- Avoided assuming consolidation fixes rivalry: yes.
- Avoided using the framework only to label good/bad industry: yes, the analysis links structure to pricing power, margins, reinvestment, and risks.
