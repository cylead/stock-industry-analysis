# ON Holding (ONON): Porter’s Five Forces Analysis

*Prepared August 12, 2026. This is a business-quality and industry-structure analysis, not investment advice or a valuation.*

## 1. Company Snapshot

**On Holding AG (NYSE: ONON)** designs and sells premium performance footwear, apparel, and accessories, primarily for running, then outdoor, training, tennis, and everyday wear. It reports one operating segment.

Source key: **ON25** = [ON FY2025 Form 20-F](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1858985/000185898526000008/onholdingag-20251231.htm); **ON22** = [ON FY2022 Form 20-F](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1858985/000185898523000011/onholdingag-20221231.htm); **NIKE25** = [Nike FY2025 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/320187/000032018725000047/nke-20250531.htm); **DECK25** = [Deckers FY2025 10-K](https://ir.deckers.com/sec-filings/all-sec-filings/content/0000910521-25-000017/0000910521-25-000017.pdf); **ADIDAS25** = [adidas FY2025 results](https://report.adidas-group.com/2025/en/at-a-glance/financial-highlights-2025.html).

- FY2025 sales were CHF3.014bn: shoes were 93.0%, apparel 5.6%, accessories 1.3% (ON25).
- Buyers: wholesale retailers/distributors generated 58.2%; direct consumers through e-commerce and owned stores generated 41.8%. The largest customer was 11% of sales; no other customer exceeded 10%.
- Geographies: Americas 57.7%, EMEA 25.3%, APAC 17.0%.
- Sellers/counterparties: substantially all products are contract-manufactured. Fewer than 30 suppliers produced ON products; six partners made about 70% of production. About 90% of footwear was made in Vietnam.
- Main cost drivers: finished-goods purchases, freight/duty, inventory provisions, third-party warehousing/logistics, marketing, owned-store rent/personnel, and athlete/endorsement contracts.

## 2. Relevant Industry Definition

The relevant market is **global premium performance running footwear and adjacent technical sportswear**, not the entire apparel market. ON competes for the consumer’s running, training, outdoor, and casual-athleisure spend, and for scarce specialty-retail shelf space.

- Main rivals: Nike, adidas, HOKA/Deckers, ASICS, Brooks, New Balance, lululemon, Anta, Li-Ning, and smaller technical brands. Nike FY2025 revenue was $46.3bn, adidas FY2025 sales €24.8bn, and HOKA FY2025 sales $2.23bn.
- Main substitutes: less-technical athletic shoes, casual footwear, continuing to use existing shoes, used footwear, and other health/fitness activities.
- Buyer mix: direct end consumers are fragmented (41.8% of ON sales); wholesale retailers/distributors are the majority channel (58.2%), making them the most important intermediaries.
- Supplier mix: contract factories, specialist textile/component mills, logistics/warehouse operators, landlords, digital platforms, and athlete/marketing partners. Exact input-spend shares are not disclosed; production concentration is the proxy.

## 6. Most Important Forces

- **Most important force:** rivalry. ON’s margin is strong, but it must continually earn consumer attention and specialty-retail space against Nike, HOKA, adidas, ASICS, Brooks, and new challengers.
- **Fastest-changing force:** rivalry. Nike’s FY2025 discounting and HOKA’s rapid expansion show how quickly the premium-running landscape can change.
- **Most misunderstood factor:** ON’s 62.8% gross margin is evidence of real brand power, but not proof of high switching costs. The purchaser can still try another shoe next season, and wholesale accounts have no long-term contracts.
- **Are profits protected?** Partly protected, but contestable. The brand, DTC growth, and product system protect current profits; supplier concentration, fashion/product-cycle risk, and rivalry mean excess returns are not automatically permanent.

## 7. Stock-Relevant Takeaways

- **Pricing power:** Good today. Rising gross margin and relatively high peer margins support a premium position; price power will be tested during broad promotions or consumer trade-down.
- **Margin durability:** Better than a commodity footwear business, but vulnerable to Vietnam sourcing, freight/tariffs, retailer demands, and discounting by larger rivals.
- **Capital intensity:** Manufacturing is asset-light, but growth still consumes inventory, store lease commitments, automated distribution investment, and marketing. This is lower-capex than owning factories, not capital-free.
- **Growth quality:** Strong: FY2025 sales grew 30%, DTC grew faster than wholesale, and apparel grew quickly. Concentration remains material: shoes are 93% of sales and the Americas are 57.7%.
- **Main risks:** loss of premium brand relevance; a HOKA/Nike/ASICS product cycle; wholesale shelf-space reductions; factory/material disruption; tariffs; inventory markdowns; and retailer conflict as DTC expands.
- **Evidence that would improve the conclusion:** continued gross-margin resilience through a promotional cycle, lower Vietnam/supplier concentration, sustained DTC mix gains without wholesale attrition, and repeatable apparel growth.
- **Evidence that would weaken it:** gross-margin declines caused by discounting, slowing full-price sell-through, rising inventory relative to sales, loss of key wholesale accounts, or an inability to dual-source core materials.
