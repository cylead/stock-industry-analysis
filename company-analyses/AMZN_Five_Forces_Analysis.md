# Amazon.com, Inc. (NASDAQ: AMZN): Porter Five Forces Analysis

- **Report date:** August 31, 2026
- **Research data cutoff:** August 31, 2026
- **Latest completed fiscal year:** 2025
- **Current-data overlay:** Quarter and six months ended June 30, 2026; regulatory developments through August 31, 2026
- **Purpose:** Business-quality, industry-structure, ecosystem-control, and profit-durability analysis. This report is informational and is **not investment advice**, a valuation, a price target, or a buy/sell recommendation.

## Executive conclusion

Amazon is better understood as a **company-controlled commerce and technology ecosystem built on top of several structurally difficult industries** than as a single online retailer. Its owned customer accounts, Prime bundle, marketplace rules, fulfillment network, purchase-intent data, advertising auctions, AWS infrastructure and APIs, devices, and content create reinforcing participation by consumers, sellers, advertisers, developers, enterprises, and creators. This control lets Amazon move economics from low-margin retail into higher-value services.

The financial split is decisive. In 2025, AWS generated only **18% of consolidated sales but 57% of operating income**. North America and International generated the other 82% of sales but only 43% of operating income. Third-party seller services, advertising, subscriptions, and AWS together produced **$419.1 billion**, or 58% of reported revenue; because third-party merchandise is recorded net, this revenue share understates the marketplace's importance. Consolidated gross margin rose from **42.0% in 2021 to 50.3% in 2025**, mostly because services grew faster than first-party merchandise. A lease-inclusive ROIC proxy averaged **12.8%** over five years and reached **16.0%** in 2025. [AMZN25] [SECFACTS]

The current direction is stronger operationally and far more capital intensive. Q2 2026 sales rose 20% to **$200.6 billion**; AWS sales rose 37% to **$42.2 billion**, and its operating margin reached **39.4%**. Yet trailing-twelve-month property purchases, net of incentives, reached **$169.0 billion**, pushing reported free cash flow to **negative $7.6 billion**. Amazon says the increase primarily reflects AI investment. High returns on the installed platform therefore coexist with a very large reinvestment claim. [AMZNQ2] [AMZNER]

**Rivalry and substitutes are the controlling forces and both exert High pressure.** Walmart, Microsoft, Google, Alibaba, TikTok, Temu, Shein, Shopify, Meta, Netflix, logistics firms, specialist AI clouds, brands selling direct, physical retail, on-premises IT, and new AI shopping interfaces attack different layers. Low consumer switching costs keep retail pricing honest; AI and social discovery weaken the assumption that product searches must start at Amazon. Conversely, network depth, an enormous fulfillment and data-center footprint, long-term AWS commitments, proprietary chips, marketplace liquidity, and accumulated trust keep the threat of broad new entry Low.

The ecosystem is strengthening economically but faces rising legal constraints. The EU already designates Amazon Marketplace and Amazon Advertising as gatekeeper services, and in June 2026 the European Commission preliminarily concluded that AWS should also be designated. The FTC's marketplace monopolization case remains unresolved; Amazon agreed to a **$2.5 billion** monetary settlement in 2025 over Prime enrollment and cancellation. On this report's cutoff date, the FTC and 22 states filed a new complaint alleging that Amazon secretly inflated advertising auction prices for more than one million brands and sellers. The latest advertising claims are **allegations, not adjudicated facts**, but they put the platform's monetization rules directly at risk. [EU-DMA] [EU-CLOUD] [FTC-MKT] [FTC-PRIME] [FTC-ADS]

Overall, Amazon's profits appear **durable but not passive**. The company controls meaningful bottlenecks and is better positioned than most peers, but preserving that position requires low consumer prices, fast delivery, large content and technology spending, continued seller value, and unprecedented AI infrastructure investment.

### Main cost drivers

The largest 2025 costs were **$356.4 billion of cost of sales**, **$109.1 billion of fulfillment**, **$108.5 billion of technology and infrastructure**, **$47.1 billion of sales and marketing**, and **$11.2 billion of general and administrative expense**. Shipping cost alone was **$102.7 billion** and total video/music expense was **$22.4 billion**. Amazon employed approximately **1.576 million** full- and part-time employees. Property purchases were $131.8 billion, and total property additions including non-cash activity were $142.4 billion. [AMZN25]

The mix matters more than any one expense line. Merchandise purchasing, transport, fulfillment labor, returns, and inventory risk dominate stores. Compute equipment, semiconductors, data centers, power, network capacity, depreciation, and technical labor dominate AWS. Content rights and production support Prime and advertising reach. Payment processing costs rise with third-party GMV even though seller revenue is recorded net.

## 2. Relevant Industry Definition

The relevant industry is **global multi-sided digital commerce and fulfillment, with adjacent hyperscale cloud, digital advertising, subscription media, devices, and selected physical retail/health/logistics services**. This scope is intentionally wider than “online retail” because the important profit pools and reinforcing participation sit across marketplace services, ads, Prime, fulfillment, AWS, and data. It is narrower than “all technology” because it follows the actual customer jobs and monetized interfaces.

- **Product/service scope:** first-party online and physical retail; third-party marketplace, FBA, and supply-chain services; purchase-intent advertising; Prime and digital content; hyperscale IaaS/PaaS/AI; devices and selected healthcare/logistics services.
- **Geographic scope:** global, with the U.S. contributing 68% of reported sales and local market structures varying materially.
- **Main direct competitors:** Walmart, Alibaba, JD, eBay, Target, Costco, MercadoLibre, Rakuten, Shopify-enabled merchants, Temu/PDD, Shein, TikTok Shop, regional retailers and grocers; Microsoft Azure, Google Cloud, Oracle, IBM, CoreWeave and other specialist clouds; Google, Meta, TikTok and retail-media networks in ads; Netflix, Disney, YouTube, Apple and other media/content services.
- **Main substitutes:** physical stores, brand/manufacturer direct-to-consumer sites, social commerce, comparison engines and AI agents, local delivery, used goods, rental, doing without a purchase, on-premises/private IT, colocation, multi-cloud, SaaS, open-source software, alternative streaming/music/content, and in-house logistics or advertising.
- **Main suppliers and counterparties:** merchandise vendors, marketplace sellers, logistics carriers and delivery partners, labor, landlords, data-center developers, chip/equipment vendors, power utilities, content and sports-rights owners, payment networks, telecoms, model providers, open-source communities, and capital providers.
- **Main buyer groups:** household consumers, business procurement customers, marketplace sellers/brands, advertisers, subscribers, developers, enterprises, governments, creators, publishers, and healthcare users.

## 3. Industry Chain and Ecosystem Control

Money flows from consumers and business buyers to Amazon or marketplace sellers; Amazon retains product margin, commissions, fulfillment and shipping fees, subscription fees, and ad spend. AWS customers pay usage, subscriptions, support, and committed-contract fees. Data flows from searches, clicks, purchases, deliveries, content consumption, ad outcomes, Alexa/devices, and cloud usage back into ranking, inventory placement, personalization, ad auctions, fraud controls, logistics, and capacity planning. Control is strongest where Amazon governs the interface and rules; it is shared where mobile operating systems, search/social discovery, merchandise suppliers, energy/chips, regulators, or enterprise software determine access and economics.

## 4. Profitability Baseline

Amazon does not report ROIC. The table therefore uses a **lease-inclusive ROIC proxy = NOPAT / average invested capital**. NOPAT is GAAP operating income multiplied by 79%, using a normalized 21% tax rate to avoid investment-gain and jurisdictional tax volatility. Year-end invested capital equals interest-bearing debt + finance-lease liabilities + operating-lease liabilities + stockholders' equity − cash − marketable securities. Adding operating leases without adding lease expense back to operating income is conservative. The calculation does not capitalize technology/R&D or advertising. [AMZN25] [AMZN22] [SECFACTS]

Amazon itself says operating income is more meaningful than gross profit because of its diverse product/service mix. Reported gross margin excludes fulfillment and most AWS infrastructure costs and rises mechanically as net-reported marketplace/services replace gross-reported first-party merchandise. It is still shown because it is the required consistent disclosed measure.

## 7. Most Important Forces

- **Which force matters most? — Rivalry.** Amazon's moat does not remove the need to compete with Walmart on price and convenience, Microsoft and Google on cloud/AI, Google/Meta/TikTok on advertising and discovery, and a long tail of regional/category specialists. Rival investment decides how much of Amazon's efficiency becomes profit versus customer value.
- **Which force is changing fastest? — Supplier power.** AI shifts the constraint toward chips, memory, power, construction, and model/talent access. Amazon's custom silicon is a meaningful defense, but AWS property additions of $90.1 billion in six months show that supply independence is capital intensive rather than free.
- **Which force is most misunderstood? — Substitutes.** Investors often compare Amazon only with another marketplace or AWS only with Azure. The real substitutes are different ways to achieve the job: a nearby store, brand site, AI agent, social feed, SaaS product, on-prem/private system, or doing nothing. The ecosystem wins by making the combination easier, not by eliminating these alternatives.
- **Are current profits protected, temporary, or likely to be competed away? — Protected but reinvestment-dependent.** Marketplace liquidity, fulfillment density, Prime, ads, cloud switching costs, custom chips, and operating learning protect substantial profit. Some incremental profit will be returned through low prices, faster delivery, credits, participant tools, compliance, and AI capex. The 2024–Q2 2026 margin strength is unlikely to be competed away quickly, but neither is it a passive annuity.

## 9. Mistakes to Avoid Check

| # | Common mistake | Check |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | Define competition only as direct competitors | **Avoided.** Included physical retail, DTC, social/AI discovery, on-prem/private cloud, SaaS, open source, content, and doing nothing. |
| 2 | Define the industry too broadly or too narrowly | **Avoided.** Defined the connected commerce/cloud/ads/subscription system while weighting segment economics separately. |
| 3 | Make lists without explaining causes using numbers | **Avoided.** Every chain, ecosystem, and force row links statistics/proxies to economic interpretation and pressure. |
| 4 | Give equal weight to every force | **Avoided.** Rivalry and substitutes are controlling; AWS receives disproportionate weight in profit durability. |
| 5 | Confuse effect with cause | **Avoided.** Price sensitivity, supplier power, switching, and rivalry are tied to concentration, alternatives, costs, differentiation, and contracts. |
| 6 | Assume fast growth means an attractive industry | **Avoided.** Growth moderates rivalry but does not remove price competition, alternatives, regulation, or capital intensity. |
| 7 | Assume advanced technology means an attractive industry | **Avoided.** AI is both a demand/differentiation driver and a source of supplier, capex, depreciation, and rivalry pressure. |
| 8 | Treat government as a sixth force | **Avoided.** Regulation is analyzed through entry cost, switching, platform conduct, buyer options, and rivalry. |
| 9 | Treat complements as a sixth force | **Avoided.** Sellers, models, creators, devices, and partners are evaluated through network effects, supply, access, substitutes, and rivalry. |
| 10 | Rely on one-year results | **Avoided.** Uses FY2021–2025 margin/ROIC evidence plus Q2 2026 operating and capital-intensity data. |
| 11 | Assume consolidation automatically fixes rivalry | **Avoided.** Oligopolistic cloud and ads still feature exceptionally capable, committed rivals and specialist entry. |
| 12 | Use the framework only to label the industry good/bad | **Avoided.** Connects structure to pricing, margin, reinvestment, growth quality, participant health, and disconfirming evidence. |
| 13 | Call vertical integration or a product suite an ecosystem without feedback loops | **Avoided.** The conclusion relies on evidenced seller-consumer-advertiser and AWS participant feedback, not breadth alone. |
| 14 | Infer control from partner counts alone | **Avoided.** Tests governance, customer access, data, switching, multi-homing, value capture, and external bottlenecks. |
| 15 | Confuse participation in another ecosystem with control | **Avoided.** Amazon controls marketplace/Prime/ads/AWS rules but is explicitly treated as dependent on mobile, search/social, chip, power, content/model, and regulatory systems. |

## Final synthesis

The underlying industry structure is **mixed and, at the commodity retail layer, unattractive**. Amazon's company position is materially better because it controls the customer interface and converts traffic, fulfillment, data, and cloud workflows into seller, advertising, subscription, and AWS economics. Relative to peers, Amazon has stronger end-to-end commerce infrastructure than digital-only platforms, stronger purchase-intent data than general ad platforms, and a larger independent cloud profit base than most infrastructure rivals. Walmart has unmatched physical-store density, Microsoft has stronger enterprise-software distribution, and Google has stronger general search/YouTube distribution and custom AI infrastructure; those advantages keep rivalry intense.

The central stock question is therefore not whether Amazon can grow. It is whether the **marginal dollar of AI, logistics, content, and ecosystem investment earns a return above the rising capital base without weakening participant economics or triggering remedies**. Sustained AWS utilization, transparent ad ROI, healthy third-party participation, rising lease-inclusive ROIC, and a return to strong free cash flow would confirm durability. Capacity excess, lower cloud margins, weakening seller/consumer engagement, external ownership of AI shopping discovery, or intrusive conduct remedies would disconfirm it.
