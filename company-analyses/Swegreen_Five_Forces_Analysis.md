## 2. Relevant industry definition

The relevant industry is narrower than “agriculture”: automated, fully controlled in-store vertical-farming systems supplied under recurring-service models to Nordic and nearby European grocery and food-service customers.

- Direct competitors include former in-store operator Infarm, technology vendors such as iFarm, LettUs Grow, Signify/GrowWise and other vertical-farm integrators.
- Nordic Harvest, Planet Farms and other centralized indoor farms compete for retailer shelf space, although their delivery model differs.
- Main substitutes are imported field-grown lettuce and herbs, domestic seasonal cultivation, conventional and high-tech greenhouses, and simply continuing existing produce procurement.
- Main suppliers are electricity and grid operators, LEDs, HVAC and irrigation equipment, sensors, seeds, nutrients, stone-wool growing media, cloud technology, specialized labour and financing.
- Main buyers are ICA franchise stores, Coop, Edeka and other grocery chains, followed by restaurants, hotels, offices and public institutions.

Swedish grocery access is unusually concentrated: 2025 market shares were ICA 50.6%, Axfood 24.9%, Coop 16.7% and Lidl 6.3%, or 98.5% combined. [Swedish Competition Authority](https://www.konkurrensverket.se/konkurrens/vagledning-inom-konkurrens/framja-etablering-av-dagligvarubutiker/kommunernas-roll-for-konkurrensen-i-dagligvaruhandeln/)

Exact revenue, order-volume and supplier proportions are **not found in public filings**. Reference-site counts and the reported cost mix are used as proxies.

## 6. Most important forces

- **Most important:** substitutes, followed closely by buyers. Conventional lettuce determines the acceptable price, while a handful of grocery groups determine access to consumers.
- **Changing fastest:** rivalry and new entry. The funding collapse and repeated bankruptcies are reducing the number of credible, heavily financed competitors.
- **Most misunderstood:** substitutes. Swegreen’s main economic competitor is not another AI vertical farm; it is an ordinary lettuce delivered through an existing retailer supply chain.
- **Profit protection:** Swegreen currently has no demonstrated profits to protect. Its technology, patent, retailer relationships and modular FaaS model are promising differentiation, but five years of negative capital returns show that the value has not yet been captured economically.
- **Industry structure:** unattractive overall. High buyer and substitute pressure place a ceiling on revenue while the vendor still bears equipment, service and financing requirements.
- **Relative position:** Swegreen appears better positioned than centralized mega-farms because it avoids distribution and large capacity additions. It is worse positioned than mature agricultural suppliers on cost, financial scale and funding resilience.

## 7. Stock-relevant takeaways

| Topic | Conclusion |
|---|---|
| Pricing power | Weak to medium. Published subscription pricing creates transparency and recurring revenue, but concentrated buyers and conventional produce cap price. Pricing secured until 2030 may improve customer adoption while limiting inflation pass-through. |
| Margin durability | Unproven. The gross-margin proxy improved after 2022, but five-year average ROIC proxy was –35.1%. No site-cohort contribution margin is disclosed. |
| Capital intensity | High for Swegreen because it retains farm ownership. This makes adoption easier for customers but shifts capital and residual-value risk to shareholders. |
| Growth quality | Mixed. 2020–24 revenue CAGR was approximately 49.6%, installations are increasing and revenue is recurring. However, EBIT remained –SEK9.0m in 2024 and other operating income represented about 10% of operating revenue. |
| Financing risk | Material. Year-end 2024 cash was SEK1.5m against SEK20.8m current liabilities. The 2026 SEK20m financing helps, but repeated capital raises or dilution remain possible until operations fund new installations. |
| Customer risk | High. Approximately 57% of reference sites are associated with ICA, and the wider buyer market is concentrated. Revenue concentration itself is not disclosed. |
| Principal operating risks | Electricity economics, installation utilization, crop sell-through, equipment reliability, customer renewal, component changes, food-safety incidents and competition from greenhouse/imported produce. |
| Evidence that would improve the conclusion | Audited 2025–26 accounts; positive contribution margin per mature installation; installation payback and utilization; crop sell-through and shrink; renewal above 90%; multi-year contract backlog; diversified revenue; positive operating cash flow and ROIC. |

## 8. Mistakes-to-avoid check

Yes—the analysis checked and avoided all twelve listed mistakes:

1. Included conventional agriculture and greenhouses, not only direct vertical-farm competitors.
2. Defined the industry around in-store vertical-farming systems, not all agriculture.
3. Used operating, financial, customer and energy proxies rather than lists alone.
4. Gave greatest weight to buyers and substitutes.
5. Explained why buyer price sensitivity exists.
6. Did not treat fast revenue growth as proof of attractiveness.
7. Did not treat AI, automation or patents as sufficient for profitability.
8. Treated regulation as affecting entry and differentiation, not as a sixth force.
9. Treated retailer infrastructure and energy integration within the five forces.
10. Used five-year accounts and multi-year industry evidence.
11. Did not assume sector consolidation automatically solves rivalry.
12. Connected the framework to pricing, capital requirements, funding risk and evidence needed from management.

No buy/sell recommendation or valuation is appropriate because Swegreen is unlisted and essential disclosures—revenue concentration, contract duration, churn, site economics and cash flow—are not public.
