# Ericsson — Five Forces Analysis

**Research date and cutoff: 2026-10-15**, including the Q3 2026 report released today. Annual history: fiscal years ended December 31, 2021–2025. All amounts are Swedish kronor unless stated otherwise. This report is for informational purposes and is **not investment advice**.

**Central judgment:** Ericsson is a strong incumbent in a mixed-to-unattractive industry. Difficult entry, embedded systems and intellectual property protect its position; concentrated customers, limited market growth and costly technology competition constrain value capture. Recent operating improvements are credible, but do not establish unrestricted pricing power or an assured new growth cycle.

## 1. Subject Snapshot

Telefonaktiebolaget LM Ericsson supplies mobile network equipment, software and services. Its shares include Nasdaq Stockholm **ERIC B** and Nasdaq US **ERIC** American depositary shares. The report uses **ERIC** for its filename and assesses the consolidated business, rather than the economics of a particular share class. [Annual report, share information][AR25].

Reported FY2025 sales by market area were Americas 35%, Europe/Middle East/Africa 30%, South East Asia/Oceania/India 12%, North East Asia 7%, and Other 16%. Other includes licensing and almost all Enterprise sales; it is **not a fifth physical region**. [Annual report, pp.1, 19][AR25].

Current context: Q3 2026 sales were **SEK 59.5 bn**, up **7% organically**; January–September organic growth was **4%** despite a reported sales decline. These are different currency/portfolio bases. Q3 reported gross margin was **47.1%**, versus adjusted gross margin of **47.7%**; “adjusted” excludes restructuring. Management warned of component-cost pressure next year. [Q3 2026, pp.1–2][Q326].

## 2. Relevant Industry Definition

The core market is **carrier-grade mobile access, core software and associated network services**, sold globally through country-specific procurement, security and spectrum regimes. Radio access networks, or RAN, connect devices to the operator's network. Core software authenticates users and manages connections. Enterprise communications software and private cellular are adjacent markets, with more fragmented buyers and stronger software/Wi-Fi alternatives.

**Peers inspected:** Nokia and Huawei. Nokia overlaps in RAN, core and services, but has substantial optical/fixed/IP businesses and separately reported patent activities in 2025. Its 2026 reorganization combines radio, core and licensing in Mobile Infrastructure, so that segment is not directly comparable with Ericsson Networks. [Nokia FY2025][N25], [2026 operating model][Nmodel], [Q2 2026][N26]. Huawei is a major global rival but its ICT Infrastructure segment also includes non-mobile activities; China exposure, ownership and export restrictions limit comparisons. Its FY2025 ICT Infrastructure revenue was CNY 375.0 bn, up 2.6%, **not a RAN market-share measure**. [Huawei 2025][H25]. Samsung and ZTE are additional relevant equipment rivals; Vonage also competes with specialist communications platforms.

Dell'Oro ranked Huawei, Ericsson, Nokia, ZTE and Samsung as the five largest RAN vendors by 2025 worldwide revenue. It reported a ten-year high in concentration. Its subsequent update puts those five vendors at **96% of worldwide RAN revenue in first-half 2026**; exact individual shares and a numerical HHI remain absent from the inspected free releases. [Q2 2026 industry update][D26]. Ericsson's claimed approximately 37% share **outside China** uses a different geographic denominator and is not its worldwide share. [Dell'Oro, February 2026][D25]; [Ericsson annual report, p.2][AR25].

Main suppliers are semiconductor designers/foundries, electronics and manufacturing providers, infrastructure/software vendors and skilled engineers. Main buyers are large operators, with private-network enterprises and public-sector agencies in adjacent markets. Supplier-spend proportions by category, named supplier and fabrication node were not found in the inspected 2025 annual report or Q3 filing; no unsupported majority is assumed. Concentrated critical inputs can matter more than their spending share.

Substitutes include retaining existing equipment longer, Wi-Fi plus fixed broadband, and satellites for selected coverage needs. Open RAN separates hardware/software interfaces and can enable **competing suppliers**; it is mainly a change in entry, switching and rivalry rather than a substitute for mobile connectivity. Enterprise network APIs have a two-sided mechanism: operators supply network capabilities, while developers/platforms purchase access. Aduna's **50% Ericsson / 50% twelve-operator ownership** is known; API transaction, supplier-revenue and developer-volume shares are not. [Aduna closing, July 2025][Aduna].

## 4. Profitability Baseline

The latest completed year is 2025; the annual report was published on **March 4, 2026**. [Publication announcement][ARrelease]. Ericsson reports ROCE, not the cash-adjusted after-tax ROIC required here. The first row therefore uses a reproducible **analytical ROIC proxy**, not relabeled reported ROCE.

| Metric | 2021 | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 | Five-year average | Notes/source |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| ROIC / return on invested capital — Ericsson, cash-adjusted after-tax proxy | 28.57% | 18.39% | −15.06% | 3.26% | 37.79% | 14.59% | Formula and inputs below. [2025 annual, pp.172–176][AR25]; [2021 annual, gross cash reconciliation][AR21]. |
| Gross margin — Ericsson, reported | 43.4% | 41.7% | 38.6% | 44.1% | 47.6% | 43.08% | Gross income / sales, including restructuring; same 2025 annual historical series, p.174. [Source][AR25]. |

**ROIC calculation:** numerator = reported EBIT minus 20.6% of positive EBIT; no hypothetical tax benefit is booked on negative EBIT. This uses the Swedish statutory rate as a common normalization, not actual multinational cash taxes. Denominator = arithmetic average of beginning/end-year **capital employed less gross cash**. Gross cash includes cash equivalents and current/noncurrent interest-bearing securities. Capital employed is total assets less disclosed non-interest-bearing provisions/liabilities. This strips treasury assets but retains goodwill and acquired intangibles until their reported impairment; it is a broad operating-capital proxy rather than a precise economic-capital estimate.

Reproducible inputs in **SEK million**, chronological: capital employed for 2020–2025 = **161990, 184283, 202899, 177965, 162967, 169176**; gross cash = **72045, 97608, 56249, 54705, 75871, 93939**. Average invested capital for 2021–2025 = **88310, 116662.5, 134955, 105178, 81166.5**; EBIT = **31780, 27020, −20326, 4313, 38634**. Sources: [2025 APM reconciliations][AR25] and [2021 reconciliation for opening gross cash][AR21]. Annual proxy results are rounded to two decimals; the average uses unrounded results. Gross-margin average uses the five displayed annual percentages.

**Limits:** the proxy is positive-denominator and comparable by formula, but not a clean measure of recurring returns. Acquisition timing makes endpoint averages approximate. Goodwill/intangible write-offs depress reported earnings and subsequently reduce capital; 2025 includes the iconectiv disposal gain. Excluding all cash also excludes any cash needed operationally, which overstates returns relative to retaining an operating cash allowance. The annual report changed ROCE's averaging method in 2025 and restated its history; the independently calculated endpoint-average proxy above uses one method throughout. [Annual report, Notes C1/H3 and APM definitions][AR25]. Do not treat the high 2025 result or five-year average as sustainable ROIC.

## 7. Structural Synthesis

**Overall structure: mixed-to-unattractive.** The supply side is concentrated and entry is difficult, but operators also concentrate purchasing power. High entry barriers protect incumbent participation more reliably than they protect industry returns. Rivalry in a slow-growing market is the most consequential force, amplified by buyer leverage; counting those as separate mechanisms does not mean adding their effects mechanically.

**Fastest-changing pressure:** suppliers. Ericsson moved from emphasizing operational improvement to explicit component-cost mitigation in Q2 2026, followed by a Q3 warning about next year's margin. Independent data-center evidence confirms strong competing demand and higher memory pricing, without measuring Ericsson's precise cost exposure. [Q2][Q226], [Q3][Q326], [Dell'Oro components][Dchip].

**Most easily misunderstood force — analytical hypothesis:** new entrants. Open RAN can lower entry barriers for components while preserving or even increasing demand for capable systems integration. AT&T's mixed-radio milestone supports both effects. It is not evidence that proprietary lock-in vanished or that Ericsson owns a permanent monopoly. [AT&T][ATT26]. This is a hypothesis about interpretation, not a measured claim about investor beliefs.

**Profit protection is partial.** Patents, deployment knowledge and integration reduce contestability between major tenders. They do not stop procurement cycles, concentrated customers or input inflation. Ericsson's margin recovery is evidence against a thesis of inevitable commoditization; Nokia's continuing investment and Huawei's scale are evidence against assuming competitors will retreat. [Ericsson FY2025][FY25], [Nokia][N25], [Huawei][H25]. Dell'Oro described Huawei as strong and Ericsson softer than expected in Q2 2026; today's stronger Ericsson Q3 is therefore not evidence of uninterrupted share gains. [Q2 industry update][D26], [Ericsson Q3][Q326]. Brand reputation supports access and perceived reliability, but its independent contribution to price or retention cannot be established from these disclosures.

On chain position, Ericsson captures engineering, systems integration, support and IP value; it controls neither the complete input stack nor subscriber monetization. Compared with Nokia, its group is more focused on operator mobile economics, while Nokia's optical/IP exposure introduces different growth drivers. Compared with Huawei, Ericsson benefits from eligibility in restricted procurement markets but lacks Huawei's China-centered commercial base. Those are conditional market-access advantages, not proof of universally superior products. [Nokia operating model][Nmodel], [Huawei][H25], [European Commission][EU].

## 8. Investment and Business Implications

**Pricing and brand:** pricing power is selective: demonstrate lifetime cost savings, software value and delivery reliability, then negotiate. A large installed base supports access and reduces replacement urgency; it does not establish a brand premium. The relevant test is realized pricing after component inflation and contract mix, not simply the announced ambition to raise prices. [Q2 management statement][Q226], [AT&T buyer objectives][ATT23].

**Margin durability:** separate cost discipline and product mix from retroactive license revenue, restructuring exclusions and divestment gains. Q3 Networks' adjusted gross margin benefited from product mix and retroactive licensing while component costs rose. That combination is consistent with a defensible core business and still leaves future hardware margins vulnerable. [Q3, Networks discussion][Q326].

**Reinvestment:** physical capital requirements understate economic reinvestment because engineering is largely expensed. Cutting R&D can improve today's cash generation while weakening the next tender cycle. Conversely, spending on 6G, AI and APIs earns a return only if customers fund deployments and profitable usage. Aduna's shared access and governance mean platform success cannot be valued as wholly controlled Ericsson network monetization. [Nokia strategic priorities][Nmodel], [Aduna][Aduna].

**Growth quality:** modest industry recovery, Ericsson share wins and recurring software/services are more concrete than treating mobile data or AI growth as automatic equipment revenue. Dell'Oro's August 2026 update describes stabilization; Nokia's radio recovery confirms competitive participation. [Dell'Oro][D26], [Nokia Q2][N26]. For ERIC shareholders, business quality depends on durable cash generation after reinvestment and Enterprise losses, rather than one unusually strong profit year.

**Evidence that would change the conclusion:** sustained multi-year equipment demand and profitable standalone/private-network adoption would improve the industry assessment; documented customer diversification would reduce buyer risk. Repeated failures to recover input inflation, loss of major installed accounts, or API/private-network spending without customer adoption would weaken the relative-position thesis. These are structural tests, not short-term trading catalysts. The final section connects them to lifecycle and valuation-method choice.

**Research boundaries:** inspected Ericsson's 2025 annual report, 2021 annual reconciliation, FY2025 results and Q2/Q3 2026 disclosures; Nokia's FY2025 and latest available Q2 2026 results; Huawei's FY2025 disclosure; and the linked buyer, industry and regulatory sources. Nokia's Q3 is scheduled for October 22, after the cutoff. [Nokia Q2, p.29][N26]. Unverified brand attribution, supplier spending and subsegment buyer proportions remain explicit gaps. ## 9. Enterprise Lifecycle, Investment Risks, and Valuation Fit

**Stage: Mature, with cyclical exposure and unproven renewal options — High confidence for the core; Medium for the group-level transition assessment.** Carrier RAN combines established cash-generating operations with repeated technology investment. New radio generations do not by themselves create a new enterprise lifecycle. The assessment uses the logic of [Damodaran's lifecycle framework][Lifecycle], with company-specific evidence below.

| Component | Assessment |
|---|---|
| Lifecycle assessment | Ericsson's core Networks and Cloud Software/Services businesses are **Mature**. The RAN contraction in 2022–2024 followed by stabilization in 2025 and modest 2026 recovery supports cyclicality rather than a proven terminal **Decline**. [Dell'Oro FY2025][D25], [Q2 2026][D26]. Enterprise contains established communications activities alongside **Early growth** network-API/private-cellular initiatives; this applies to initiatives, not to the entire segment. The June/July 2025 formation of shared API distribution capacity supports an opportunity, but not mature platform economics. [Aduna closing][Aduna]. High core confidence reflects observable market history; Medium group-transition confidence reflects uncertain adoption and monetization. |
| Investment risks | **1. Mature-market cash erosion:** operator concentration and renewed input inflation can squeeze spreads even when order volumes recover; this is business risk, not merely reported currency movement. [Q2 statement][Q226], [Q3 outlook][Q326]. **2. Renewal and capital allocation:** funding Enterprise, APIs and next-generation networks before returns are demonstrated can destroy value. Acquisition-related impairments and the iconectiv sale make reported capital returns difficult to extrapolate. [Annual, Notes C1/E2][AR25]. **3. Earnings normalization and valuation sensitivity:** investors could capitalize temporary licensing, mix or disposal benefits as permanent cash flow. Mature economics provide less growth to repair that error. At September 2026, SEK 60.7 bn net cash reduces near-term financing dependence, but is not a guarantee against acquisitions, restructuring or excess distributions. [Q3 financial position][Q326]. |
| Valuation fit | **Normalized P/E** is a suitable multiple for the profitable core/group only after reconciling disposal gains, impairments, taxes and a realistic recurring restructuring burden. A low multiple on unusually high earnings can mislead. **Discounted cash flow** is a suitable method using full-cycle operator spending and separate scenarios for Enterprise/API adoption; it should include engineering expense, working capital, capital expenditure and failure cases. Terminal growth and margins require particular discipline in a mature market. **Equity free-cash-flow yield** is a useful cross-check when cash available to shareholders is defined after capital expenditure, lease/debt cash obligations as appropriate, and normalized working capital. Ericsson's disclosed “free cash flow before M&A” is a starting reconciliation item, not automatically distributable owner cash. None of these methods is calculated here. |
| Transition indicators | **1. Demand:** several years of broad organic growth with operator funding and profitable deployment, rather than a single region or contract, would support **Maturing growth** at group level. **2. Core economics:** resilient margins and cash conversion through higher component costs and renewed tender competition would strengthen the mature-quality thesis; persistent erosion would raise a decline hypothesis. **3. Renewal discipline:** independently visible paid API/private-network adoption, improving Enterprise recurring economics and reinvestment funded by internally generated cash would support renewal. Expansion driven mainly by acquisitions or spending would not. [Q3 results and segment commentary][Q326], [Aduna operating model][Aduna]. |

Capital allocation should therefore be judged against the mature core's ongoing engineering needs and the uncertain payoff of expansion. Q3's share repurchases demonstrate cash returns, while the balance sheet provides capacity to invest; neither proves that the next investment or buyback creates value. [Q3 capital-return and cash-flow disclosures][Q326]. A lasting reclassification would require evidence that new uses expand profitable demand enough to overcome mature-RAN constraints. Until then, the appropriate conclusion is a defensible incumbent with meaningful competition and allocation risk, without an assertion about whether today's share price is attractive.
