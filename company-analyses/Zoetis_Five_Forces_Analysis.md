# Zoetis: Five Forces Industry Analysis

**Company:** Zoetis Inc. (NYSE: ZTS)  
**Information cutoff:** July 19, 2026  
**Financial period:** FY2021–FY2025, with Q1 2026 used only as a current trading signal  
**Currency:** U.S. dollars unless noted  

## Bottom line

The global animal-health industry is structurally attractive for scaled innovators, but it is becoming less forgiving at the product level. Regulation, specialized R&D, trusted brands, manufacturing know-how, and veterinarian access keep the threat from new entrants low. Those protections show up in Zoetis's five-year average gross margin of **70.5%** and estimated average ROIC of **28.0%**.

The main pressure is rivalry, followed by buyer and channel power. Global competitors are well funded, patent expirations are opening selected franchises to generics, and pet owners are more price sensitive. Zoetis nevertheless retains meaningful defenses: a broad portfolio, direct technical selling, global scale, and products whose efficacy and safety matter more than a small price difference. The industry verdict is therefore **attractive, but increasingly franchise-specific** rather than uniformly protected.

## 1. Company snapshot

Zoetis is a global animal-health company spanning medicines, vaccines, diagnostics, biodevices, genetic tests, and precision-animal-health products. It sells in more than 100 countries and directly markets in approximately 45. Its commercial focus covers eight core species and seven major product categories. [ZTS25]

FY2025 revenue was **$9.467 billion**. Companion animal represented **69.6%**, livestock **29.2%**, and contract manufacturing/human health **1.2%**. Geographically, the U.S. contributed **53.8%** and international markets **44.9%**, with the remainder from contract manufacturing and human health. The three largest categories—parasiticides, vaccines, and dermatology—accounted for **64.0%** of revenue. [ZTS25]

Zoetis's largest disclosed customer, a U.S. veterinary distributor, represented **16%** of FY2025 revenue. No supplier accounted for a disclosed percentage of purchases; supplier concentration by spend was **Not found in public filings**. Zoetis instead discloses that some inputs are sole- or exclusive-sourced and that its network includes 21 owned manufacturing sites and more than 90 contract manufacturers. [ZTS25]

The main cost drivers are regulated manufacturing and materials, a technically trained global sales organization, research and development, promotion, and capital needed for validated production capacity. In FY2025, cost of sales was **28.2%** of revenue, SG&A **25.1%**, and R&D **7.4%**. [ZTS25]

Q1 2026 showed the present tension beneath the strong FY2025 economics: revenue rose **3% reported** but declined **1% operationally**. U.S. companion-animal sales fell **11%**, with management citing softer end demand, greater competition, macroeconomic price sensitivity, generic Cerenia and Convenia, and lower Librela sales. [ZTSQ126]

## 2. Relevant industry definition

**Industry analyzed:** Global animal-health therapeutics, preventives, vaccines, and clinical diagnostics sold for companion animals and livestock.

This boundary is wider than prescription pharmaceuticals because Zoetis competes for the same veterinarian, producer, and pet-owner budgets through vaccines, diagnostics, devices, parasiticides, and other health products. It excludes pet food, veterinary-practice ownership, and general livestock equipment except where they act as substitutes or influence channel power.

**Customers and channels:** For companion animals, manufacturers sell mainly through veterinarians and distributors, with pet owners as the ultimate payer; retail pharmacies and e-commerce also participate. For livestock, the customers are veterinarians, producers, distributors, and retailers. Exact direct-versus-distributor revenue proportions were **Not found in public filings**. [ZTS25]

**Participants:** The principal competitors are Zoetis, Merck Animal Health, Boehringer Ingelheim Animal Health, Elanco, and—especially in diagnostics—IDEXX, plus hundreds of smaller firms and startups. Main substitutes include approved generics, compounded and over-the-counter products, nutrition or behavior interventions, surgery, preventive care, and doing nothing or delaying treatment. Supplier groups include active ingredients, biologic materials, contract manufacturers, packaging, equipment, logistics, and specialized scientific labor. Zoetis's revenue mix is the best public proxy for counterparties served: companion-animal buyers are the majority at **69.6%**, livestock buyers are the minority at **29.2%**, and other activities are **1.2%**. Supplier proportions and direct-versus-intermediated sales were **Not found in public filings**. [ZTS25]

**Market structure:** HealthforAnimals reported **$38.6 billion** of 2024 revenue for the 20 largest animal-health companies and approximately **9% average annual sector growth** over 2014–2023. A directional four-firm concentration proxy—Zoetis, Merck Animal Health, Boehringer Ingelheim Animal Health, and Elanco—is about **67%** of that market measure. This is not a formal CR4: it mixes FY2025 company sales with a 2024 industry denominator, converts Boehringer's euros at its reported 2025 average rate, and does not perfectly align product boundaries. A reliable industry HHI was **Not found in public filings**. [HFA_VALUE] [MRK25] [BI25] [ELAN25]

## 6. The most important force

**Most important — rivalry.** Entry barriers protect the industry from outsiders, but they do not protect Zoetis from Merck, Boehringer, Elanco, IDEXX, or well-funded specialists already inside the regulatory perimeter. These competitors can match investment, use global channels, and focus on the same attractive indications.

This force also changes the effect of the others:

- A sole-source constraint matters more when a competitor can take share during a shortage.
- Buyer power rises when several credible therapies or generics give veterinarians alternatives.
- Substitutes become economically significant when rivals validate a different care pathway.
- High entry barriers support profit only until an established competitor or a funded biotech clears them.

**Changing fastest — buyer power.** The fastest-moving evidence is in U.S. companion animal, where Q1 2026 combined softer demand, price sensitivity, generic entry, heightened competition, and a franchise-specific safety issue. Owners have more information and fulfillment options, while veterinary groups and distributors can aggregate their influence.

**Most misunderstood — buyer power.** Millions of fragmented owners make the end market look powerless, but veterinarians decide many prescriptions and distributors aggregate purchases. The relevant buyer system is therefore more concentrated than the population of owners suggests.

**Are current profits protected?** The platform-level profit pool is substantially protected by regulation, manufacturing, clinical evidence, brands, and channels. Individual product profits are temporary when patents expire, safety confidence changes, or a rival creates a better therapy. Excess returns are likely to persist for leaders, but must be renewed rather than treated as an annuity.

## 7. Stock-analysis takeaways

This is an industry and business-quality assessment, not a buy/sell recommendation.

1. **Durability is supported, not guaranteed.** Five years of approximately 70% gross margins and high-20s estimated ROIC are consistent with structural advantages. Investors should separate those platform economics from the outlook for each large franchise.
2. **Portfolio concentration is the key bridge from industry to equity risk.** The three largest categories supply about 64% of revenue and the leading product lines 71%. A patent, safety, reimbursement, or competitive event can therefore matter despite species and geographic breadth.
3. **Pricing quality should be judged with volume.** FY2025's 4% price contribution was strong, but Q1 2026 operational revenue declined as U.S. companion volume weakened. Price-led growth is less valuable if access, visits, or unit share erode.
4. **The channel deserves explicit monitoring.** A single distributor is 16% of revenue, while veterinarians influence both prescribing and fulfillment. Watch customer concentration, days in channel, direct-to-consumer pharmacy shifts, and veterinary-group consolidation.
5. **Innovation productivity matters more than R&D quantity.** Zoetis's 300-plus programs and $698 million budget are advantages only if approvals expand markets or defend premium positions. Track approval cadence, new-product contribution, label changes, and competitive readouts.
6. **Manufacturing is both moat and risk.** Owned, validated capacity raises entry barriers, but sole-source inputs and single-site products can turn a disruption into lost share. Inventory growth and service levels should be read together.
7. **Companion animal and livestock have different cyclicality.** Companion animal offers secular pet-care demand but is exposed to self-pay affordability and visit trends. Livestock demand is tied more directly to producer economics, disease pressure, trade, and protein cycles.
8. **Regulatory safety is an economic variable.** FDA's Librela label changes and reported adverse events illustrate how pharmacovigilance can alter veterinarian confidence and revenue well after approval. Reported events do not by themselves establish causality, but commercial impact can precede scientific resolution. [FDA_LIBRELA]

## Final synthesis

The industry structure is **attractive for scaled innovators but mixed for undifferentiated products**. Rivalry is the controlling force, with buyer power changing fastest. Zoetis is better positioned than smaller peers against entry, suppliers, and distribution because of its breadth, owned capacity, patent estate, and field organization; versus the other global leaders, its advantage depends more on portfolio quality and execution.

| New entrants | **Low** | **Worsening** | Zoetis is better protected than a narrow peer by its platform, but outsourced development makes targeted attacks easier. |
| Suppliers | **Medium** | **Stable** | Owned scale is favorable versus smaller peers; sole-source or single-site dependencies can still turn disruption into lost share. |
| Buyers | **Medium** | **Worsening** | Zoetis's breadth helps in the channel, but veterinarian influence, distributor concentration, and self-pay affordability constrain pricing and volume. |
| Substitutes | **Medium** | **Worsening** | Premium innovation can defend share; mature franchises face generics and alternative care paths after exclusivity. |
| Rivalry | **Medium** | **Worsening** | Zoetis has the largest sales base and strong margins, but other global leaders can match investment, making portfolio renewal essential. |

The conclusion would be wrong if entry timelines and development costs fall much faster than expected, veterinary gatekeepers lose influence, generics erode brands broadly before patent expiry, or Zoetis cannot convert its R&D and capacity investment into volume-led growth. The monitoring table in Section 7 makes those disconfirming signals explicit.
